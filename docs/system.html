Colour

Neutral ramp · L0

Ten ordinal steps, warm at H 85, shared by both exposures. They do not flip: n-01 is ink and n-10 is paper on either ground. Warm because cool darks against warm paper made the two exposures read as different families.

Surfaces

What the ramp is actually for. Four levels — page, card, popover, dialog. Editorial has only 0.063 of lightness between paper and white, so all four share it evenly rather than three eating it and a fourth scraping in. Ink has room, so its four are simply ramp steps 01–04, with rungs that widen going up because a fixed lightness step is less visible the darker it is.

The role scale · 10 hues × 8 steps

Each step is named for the job it does, not for how light it is. This is what lets a whole component be built out of one hue without improvising: bg + line + text is a complete tinted panel.

bg · bg2 a wash you can set body copy on, and its hover line · line2 hairline, and emphasised solid · solid2 the mark — seen, not read — and its hover text · text2 type at AA, and above

One hue, one component

The same panel in four bands. No color-mix, no per-band CSS — each panel just declares [data-band] and reads --rsk-bg, --rsk-line, --rsk-tint, --rsk-mark.

The four bands

A band is a named position on the spectrum with a meaning attached. Rule 1: a band never appears decoratively — if something is amber it is about interface.

Reserved · status is not a band

620nm and 700nm are excluded from every categorical set, so an alarm can never be mistaken for a classification. Status carries one hue and encodes the rest in form.

ok — hollow, no hue watch — ring critical — filled, 700nm

Chart series

Six, in dispersion order. Six is where every stop sits at its own gamut ceiling and nothing collides — past six, label the series directly.

Syntax

Its own solve, against the code block's ground rather than the page's. Roles follow the band taxonomy: keywords take intelligence, strings systems, numbers interface, functions compute.

The spectrum re-solves

Instrument Serif

The serif is gated on voice, not exposure — a dark site that publishes essays is luminous and authored. Without data-voice="author" the serif stack resolves to the sans, so product surfaces cannot leak it.

Prose sits back so emphasis can step forward

Body copy runs at --rsk-text-prose, about 10:1 — comfortably past AAA, and deliberately not maximum. That leaves <strong> somewhere to go: full contrast at 16.5:1, a 1.65× jump. Set the paragraph at maximum and bold has nothing but weight, which at 500 against 400 is a whisper.

The mark ring is shared between exposures — a vivid mark is a vivid mark on either ground. Only the text ring re-solves, pulled back until it passes AA against whatever it is standing on. That asymmetry is the whole argument: colour that is seen and colour that is read answer to different constraints, and pretending otherwise is what made the earlier ring look like mud.

Geometry

Rule 7: radius encodes role. Structure holds other things and gets the hard corner. Tokens are things you could pick up and get the pill. The test — does it contain something, or is it a thing?

Rules and depth

There are no shadows. A hairline derived from --rsk-text at low alpha composites with whatever sits behind it, so the same border reads darker on a raised surface than on the ground — depth, rather than an outline drawn on top. On ink, emission does the job instead.

Marks are seen; text is read

Never swap the two rings. A mark exists to be found at a glance and pays no attention to WCAG text ratios; a tint exists to be read and is constrained to AA against its own ground. The two share a hue angle so they read as one colour, and nothing else.

A component that needs a [data-exposure] override is a bug in the tokens.
