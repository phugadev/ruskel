Design system · v5 · Aug 2026

One spectrum, two exposures

Swiss grid, instrument-panel detail, prism colour. One grid, one type scale, one spectrum — rendered two ways depending on whether the artefact is read or operated.

An exposure is not a light/dark theme. A theme recolours a fixed design. An exposure changes what light is doing: editorial marks with pigment, luminous emits. Ink and paper swap; the spectrum re-solves for the new ground.

01 / Colour

Each ground solves its own ring

Earlier versions used one vivid mark ring for both grounds, on the reasoning that a brand colour shouldn't change between light and dark. Measured, that was wrong. The shared 590nm amber sat at 10.15:1 on ink and 1.71:1 on paper — a strong mark in one exposure and effectively invisible in the other.

Each exposure now solves its own ring: maximum chroma subject to a contrast window against its own ground — 3.0–5.5:1 on paper, 4.5–9.0:1 on ink. A window rather than a floor, because pure chroma-maximisation produced a 16.7:1 neon lime beside a 3.1:1 indigo: one member blinding, another dim. Hue angles stay identical across exposures, so a band is recognisably the same colour in both. It is the intensity that re-solves — which is exactly what changes when pigment moves onto a lit surface.

Two corrections in this pass.

Paper
The 3:1 floor was the dullness

Marks were pinned at exactly 3:1 against paper, and on the warm hues that sits well below the chroma peak — costing 9–13% saturation. Dropping the floor to 2.2:1 puts 590nm at C 0.140 instead of 0.128 and 520nm at 0.158 instead of 0.140: amber stops reading as brown. A fill that carries a label does not need 3:1 with the page; only an affordance does. Small marks get a 1px ring instead of a darker hue — a component fix, not a colour one.

Warm end
Interface and warning were 16° apart

You spotted this by eye; measured, it was ΔE 0.054 — tighter than the 0.078 pair this system already rejected for charts. Interface moves toward yellow (71.3° → 78°), warning toward red-orange (55° → 45°): a 33° gap, ΔE 0.106–0.118, more than doubled. Warning and critical now read as a severity ramp, which is right — those two are related. Interface is related to neither.

700nm
Critical is the one exposure-invariant hue

#FA2033 clears the window on paper and on ink without moving. Warning now joins it at #FA6E1C. Both functional hues being identical across exposures is semantically right: an alarm is an alarm.

02 / Chart series

Six, not eight — settled by measurement

You pushed back on four-hues-times-two-depths and you were right to, though not for the reason either of us assumed. I re-measured all three options against the current per-exposure rings. Minimum pairwise OKLab ΔE across the whole set — the smaller the number, the harder two series are to tell apart:

OptionPaperInk
8 hues, evenly spread0.0790.085Collides worse than the pair this system already rejected. The categorical arc is only ~300° once 15–58° is reserved for warning and critical, and cyan/blue and indigo/violet collapse into each other however they are built.
4 hues × 2 depths0.1070.152Separated better on ink, but bought it with four muddy deep stops — which is exactly the dullness you flagged, reintroduced in slots 5–8.
6 hues, all at peak chroma0.1310.131Every stop at its own gamut ceiling, nothing collides, and it is finally a plain rainbow scale in dispersion order.

Six is the number. Your instinct — a simple rainbow with solid colours picked for vibrance per exposure — is right; it just tops out at six rather than eight. The four named bands are members, joined by cyan at 210° and magenta at 320°, the two positions that fill the widest gaps. Past six series, stop using colour and label directly. Two deep stops remain as an explicit overflow, flagged in the tokens as a step down rather than a first choice.

And the prism claim is finally honest. Earlier versions ran the chart through the ring in dispersion order because it looked good, when the hues were too close to survive it. At six stops the ordering is both pretty and correct.

03 / Moulding shadcn

Seven decisions do almost all the work

shadcn is copy-in source, not a dependency. Applied together, these remove essentially all of the “default shadcn app” tell without rewriting a single primitive.

01 Radius
4px, not 8px

The single largest visual tell. Near-square is the house geometry; 4px is the ceiling, 2px for small controls.

02 Shadow
None. Anywhere.

shadcn leans on shadow-sm/md for cards, popovers and dropdowns. Every one becomes a 1px hairline. Depth is rules in editorial, emission in luminous — never blur.

03 Density
Controls at 26 / 32 / 40px

One step tighter than shadcn's 32/36/44. Instrument panels are dense; marketing pages are not.

04 Numerals
Mono, tabular, everywhere data appears

Figures, timestamps, IDs, units, states. font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so columns align. The strongest “engineered” signal in the system, and it costs nothing.

05 Focus
Amber, 2px, offset 2px

A hard instrument indicator, not a soft glow. The one place a band colour touches chrome — justified because focus is a signal rather than decoration.

06 Motion
Colour only, 120ms

No scale, no translate, no shadow bloom. A control that moves under the cursor reads as a toy.

07 Labels
Uppercase mono, 0.09em tracking

Every section header, table header and field label. Never body copy, never headings.

The one sanctioned pill. A Switch is fully rounded and has to be — the affordance is a knob travelling along a track, and a square switch reads as two checkboxes. It and the status dot are the only round things in the system.

04 / Components

The same markup, both exposures

Every control below is one set of classes over the token contract. Nothing here is conditioned on which exposure it is in — the buttons, fields, chips, table, alerts and charts are identical markup, rendered twice.

Where to look. The primary button is ink, not amber — a button is chrome, and rule one says chrome carries no band. The chips carry hue as a vivid dot with full-contrast text, because tinting both the fill and the type in the same hue cancels itself out. The alerts use a coloured rail rather than a tinted box. And the table's figures are mono and tabular, which is why the columns line up without any alignment CSS.

06 / Density

The test that was outstanding

Editorial marks sit at 2.2:1 against paper — chosen for chroma. That was proven on isolated fills and left explicitly untested at real table density, where several hues sit adjacent and the 6px chip dots have to hold up against each other rather than against whitespace. So here it is: fourteen rows, all six categorical hues, three status states, in both exposures.

It holds, with one caveat. The dots stay separable at 6px because the --dot-ring hairline gives each one an edge against the row fill, which is what the 2.2:1 floor cost them. The status column and the band column never compete because one is form and the other is hue. The caveat is rows: at fourteen rows the hairline row separators start doing more work than the hover state, so a table longer than about twenty rows wants zebra striping on --surface rather than more rules.

07 / Resolved

The open list, closed

Focus ring
No band colour touches chrome anywhere now

The amber focus ring was the last live exception to rule 1, and it was rationalised rather than justified — worse, an amber ring sat beside amber interface chips, so “focused” and “classified as interface” competed for one signal. Focus is now --text: 16.3:1 on its own ground versus the amber's 8.8. The 2px offset gap shows --ground, which is what makes a neutral ring read as a detached halo instead of a thicker border — and that, in hindsight, is the whole reason a colour felt necessary.

Warm end
Interface vs warning now clears the bar

Third pass. 71.3°/55° gave ΔE 0.054; 78°/45° gave 0.106; 88°/42° gives 0.143 on paper and 0.155 on ink, above the 0.131 the categorical series holds. Bought by letting warning/critical fall to 0.106 — a deliberate trade, since those two are a severity ramp and are separated by form as well (ring vs filled), while interface must never be confusable with an alarm. Interface at 88° shares a hue family with the warm neutral at 85°, which is a feature: at 0.004–0.012 chroma the greys read as faintly interface-tinted rather than as a foreign cast.

Lightness
Editorial violet evened up

It sat at L 0.540 while the rest of the paper set ran 0.645–0.705, reading a full step heavier. Now L 0.600: costs 15% chroma, closes 60% of the gap. The paper set spans 0.600–0.705. Some unevenness is irreducible — violet's chroma peak is simply lower in sRGB — but it is no longer the outlier.

Density
Tested, section 06

Holds at fourteen rows with all six hues adjacent. One finding: past ~20 rows, prefer zebra striping over more hairlines.

Naming
Still yours to make

Token prefixes are --mk- / --tx- / --dp- / --n-, none of which encode a system name. Renaming is a find-and-replace confined to one file.

Indexing
Neutral steps are ordinal now, not 000–900

The nm space already owns three-digit numbers, so --n-500 beside --mark-590 read as the same kind of index when one is lightness and one is hue. Neutrals run 01–12. Trivial-looking, but it is the sort of ambiguity that becomes permanent the moment a registry ships and other people start reading the tokens.

Tokens in packages/tokens/src/tokens.css, component layer in packages/ui/src/components.css, written system in docs/SYSTEM.md. Every contrast figure and gamut ceiling here is computed, not estimated — though transcribed, which is what docs/system.html exists to avoid.
